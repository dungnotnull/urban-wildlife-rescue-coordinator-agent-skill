## 1. Core Concepts & Frameworks

### 1.1 Foundational Methods in Urban Wildlife Rescue & Rehabilitation

#### Triage
- **Injured vs Orphaned vs Conflict**: First decision point. Injured animals → veterinary referral. True orphans (confirmed maternal death/abandonment) → rehabilitation. Healthy juveniles found alone → monitor before intervention (avoid "kidnapping" healthy wildlife).
- **Species behavior assessment**: Nocturnal species found during day may indicate illness. Prey species showing no fear response may indicate neurological impairment.
- **Intervention necessity test**: Is the animal in immediate danger? Is there confirmed injury? Has maternal abandonment been verified (≥4 hours observation for most mammals, ≥12 hours for lagomorphs)?

#### Capture, Handling & Transport
- **Species-specific restraint**: Raptors → control feet first (talons are primary defense). Mammals → species-appropriate nets, catch-poles, or live traps. Reptiles → support full body, secure head for venomous species.
- **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)**: Minimum: puncture-resistant gloves (Hexarmor or equivalent), eye protection. Rabies-vector species (raccoons, skunks, foxes, bats) → full-face shield + bite-proof gauntlets. Herpes B risk (macaques) → full barrier precautions.
- **Zoonosis precautions**: Rabies (lyssavirus), Herpes B (Cercopithecine herpesvirus 1), Salmonella (reptiles/amphibians), Psittacosis (Chlamydia psittaci from birds), Hantavirus (rodents), Dermatophytosis (ringworm from mammals).
- **Transport protocol**: Warm (supplemental heat for neonates/hypothermic), dark (covered carrier reduces stress), quiet (no radio, minimal handling). Ventilated but escape-proof container. No food/water during short transports (<2 hours).

#### Rehabilitation & Release
- **Minimum Standards for Wildlife Rehabilitation** (IWRC, 2018): Species-appropriate diet, enclosure size meeting natural movement needs, behavioral conditioning for wild behaviors, minimal human imprinting.
- **Release criteria**: Self-feeding independently, species-appropriate weight, recognizes and avoids predators (where applicable), normal locomotion and flight (for birds), waterproof plumage (for waterbirds), appropriate fear of humans.
- **Soft release**: Gradual transition using outdoor pre-release enclosures at release site with supplemental feeding tapered over 1–4 weeks.
- **Hard release**: Direct release at suitable habitat when animal meets all criteria; best for adult animals with limited rehabilitation time.

#### Coexistence & Human-Wildlife Conflict Mitigation
- **Exclusion**: One-way doors for attic-dwelling wildlife (bats, raccoons, squirrels), chimney caps, soffit repair, fence barriers for ground-level entry.
- **Deterrents**: Motion-activated sprinklers, ultrasonic devices (limited efficacy), predator urine, habitat modification (removing food attractants).
- **Permits and legal compliance**: CITES for endangered species, Migratory Bird Treaty Act (US), Wildlife and Countryside Act (UK), local/state wildlife agency permits for possession, transport, and release.
- **Rescuer and transport coordination**: Dispatch protocols, volunteer networks, wildlife hotlines, coordination with animal control and law enforcement.

## 3. State-of-the-Art Methods & Tools

### 3.1 Emerging Technologies
- **AI-powered species identification from photographs**: Deep learning models (ResNet, EfficientNet) trained on iNaturalist and Macaulay Library datasets achieving >95% accuracy for common urban species. Enables remote triage from citizen reports.
- **Rescue dispatch mobile applications**: GPS-enabled apps (Animal Help Now, Wildlife Rescue) connecting the public with nearest licensed rehabilitators in real-time.
- **Tele-veterinary triage**: Remote video consultation for initial assessment, reducing unnecessary transports and stress.
- **Zoonosis risk models**: Bayesian network models integrating species, geography, season, and clinical presentation to estimate zoonotic risk probability.

## 5. Analytical Frameworks

### 5.1 Rescue Triage Decision Tree
```
REPORT RECEIVED
├── Species identified?
│   ├── YES → Assess conservation status (protected/threatened/least concern)
│   └── NO  → Request photo, use AI species ID, consult expert
├── Condition assessed?
│   ├── Injured → Veterinary referral mandatory → Gate G1
│   ├── Orphaned → Verify maternal absence → If orphaned: rehab → If unsure: monitor 4–24h
│   ├── Conflict → Assess threat level → Exclusion/deterrent vs relocation
│   └── Healthy → Release or monitor — "do not kidnap healthy wildlife"
├── Capture feasible?
│   ├── YES → Species-specific capture protocol + PPE + zoonosis precautions → Gate G2
│   └── NO  → Monitor, engage specialized rescue team, coordinate with authorities
└── Disposition?
    ├── Rehabilitate → Transfer to licensed facility → Gate G3
    ├── Release on-site → Suitable habitat check, release criteria met → Gate G3
    └── Euthanasia → Only when prognosis is grave and suffering cannot be alleviated (veterinary decision)
```

### 5.2 Zoonosis Risk Assessment Matrix
| Species Group | High-Risk Pathogens | PPE Level | Handling Protocol |
|---------------|---------------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Mammals (carnivores) | Rabies, Baylisascaris, Leptospira | Level 3: bite-proof gloves, face shield, rabies vax | Restraint by experienced handler; avoid face proximity |
| Mammals (rodents) | Hantavirus, LCMV, Salmonella | Level 2: puncture-resistant gloves, N95 mask | Capture with live trap; minimize aerosol from droppings |
| Mammals (bats) | Rabies, Histoplasma | Level 3: bite-proof gloves, face shield, rabies vax | Only vaccinated personnel handle; double-bag for transport |
| Birds | Chlamydia psittaci, Salmonella, avian influenza | Level 2: gloves, N95 mask, eye protection | Minimize feather dust; quarantine from other birds |
| Reptiles | Salmonella, Cryptosporidium | Level 1: gloves, hand hygiene | No mouth-pipetting; dedicated equipment |
| Amphibians | Salmonella, Chytrid fungus | Level 1: gloves, hand hygiene | Prevent cross-contamination between enclosures |
| Primates (non-human) | Herpes B, TB, Hepatitis | Level 4: full barrier, trained personnel only | Immediate transfer to primate-qualified facility |
